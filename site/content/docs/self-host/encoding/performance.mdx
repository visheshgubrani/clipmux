title: Encoding performance
description: Measured encode and transcription times on Modal GPUs and a local AMD card, and what drives them.
---

These are measurements of real ClipMux runs, with the hardware and the date
of each. They aren't guarantees: your sources, GPUs and network will differ.
Use them to choose where to encode and to estimate how long a library takes.

## What drives encode time

- **The source's resolution and codec.** A 4K source encodes about 4.3
  times the pixels of a 1080p one, and gets two more renditions (2160p and
  1440p). A codec the GPU can't decode, such as AV1 on a T4, decodes on the
  CPU instead (`nvenc-hybrid`), which is much slower.
- **Which renditions you ask for.** By default every standard rendition up
  to the source's size is encoded. Asking for fewer encodes less. See
  [Choose renditions](/docs/build/upload/renditions).
- **GPU or CPU.** A GPU path decodes, scales and encodes on the card. The CPU
  path encodes with libx264, at idle priority on this server.
- **Uploading the output.** A long video makes thousands of files. The
  encoder uploads 32 at a time.
- **Subtitles.** Transcription is its own job after the encode, and on a
  long video it can take as long as the encode.
- **Cloud start-up.** A Modal machine took 20 to 53 seconds to start after
  an upload finished.

## Three videos on Modal

Measured November 2026 on Modal, with the default sandbox (4 CPU cores,
16 GiB), three lectures uploaded back to back with up to 10 machines at
once. Each got its own machine with no queue wait, and every job succeeded
first time. "Upload to subtitles" is from the end of the upload until the
subtitles were ready.

| Video | Source | GPU | Video encode | Output upload | Audio fetch | Transcription (realtime factor) | Upload to subtitles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 min | 1080p VP9 | T4 | 207 s | 41 s (998 files, 207 MB) | 12 s | 162 s (0.14) | 8 min |
| 54 min | 4K AV1, 6 renditions | L4 | 1,086 s | 121 s (3,832 files, 5.9 GB) | 31 s | 763 s (0.23) | 35 min |
| 67 min | 1080p AV1 | T4, `nvenc-hybrid` | 1,372 s | 143 s (3,358 files, 5.2 GB) | 42 s | 1,196 s (0.30) | 48 min |

The 67-minute AV1 video on the T4 spent 23 minutes on video, because the T4
can't decode AV1 and the machine's four CPUs decoded it. A 76-minute 1080p
AV1 video on an L4 spent 6 minutes. That is why the L4 and A10G are Modal's
default GPUs.

## Transcription

The realtime factor is transcription time divided by the audio's length:
0.23 means 10 minutes of audio took 2.3 minutes.

- **On Modal with CUDA** (November 2026), whisper.cpp ran at 0.14 on an
  English lecture and 0.23 to 0.30 on Hindi lectures, in the table above.
- **The 14-second JFK test clip**, under the sandbox with CUDA, took 5.2 s on
  an L4 and 4.3 s on a T4, including loading the model.
- **On an AMD RX 6600 XT with Vulkan**, under the sandbox, the same clip
  took 2.7 s, against 22.8 s on the CPU.

## Short 4K clips

A 2-second 4K clip, encoded into every rendition, measures the fixed cost of
an encode more than its speed:

| Hardware | Path | Time for 2 s of 4K |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AMD RX 6600 XT, local, November 2026 | `vaapi` | 2.3 s |
| NVIDIA L4 and T4 on Modal, November 2026 | `nvenc` | about 45 to 50 s |

The gap points to a large fixed cost per encode on Modal's runtime, which a
short clip shows and a long video spreads out; the Modal table above is the
better guide for real videos.
