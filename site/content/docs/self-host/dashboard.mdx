The dashboard is where the owner runs the instance: videos, encoding,
storage, delivery, captions and the apps that use the API. It is an API
client like any other, with no private path into the server, so everything
it does can also be done through the
[management API](/docs/reference/management-api). It works in light and dark
themes, on a laptop or a phone.

The sidebar has two groups: **Home**, **Videos**, **Analytics** and
**Jobs**, then **Encoding**, **Storage**, **Delivery**, **Captions & AI**
and **Apps & API**, with **Settings** at the bottom. The menu under your
email holds the theme switch, **Settings** and **Sign out**.

## Home

A summary line and an **Upload** button, then:

- **Finish setting up**: the optional steps left (store uploads in a
  bucket, connect Cloudflare, add a cloud GPU, turn on chapters, create an
  app), until they're done or you hide them.
- **Needs attention**: videos that failed or are still encoding, with what
  to do.
- **Recent videos**, and a status line for encoding, delivery, uploads and
  captions, each linking to its page.

## Videos

![The Videos page: a grid of posters with a search box and status filters](/screenshots/videos-light.webp)

Every video, as a poster grid or a list. Search by title, reference or ID,
filter by status (**All**, **Ready**, **Encoding**, **Failed**) and, when
there's more than one, by app. Drop files anywhere on the page to upload
them.

## A video

![A video's page: the player with subtitles, and the video's details](/screenshots/video-light.webp)

Opening a video shows four tabs:

- **Overview**: the player, a chapter strip under it when the video has
  chapters, and the details (duration, privacy, app, resolutions, captions,
  ID and reference).
- **Captions & chapters**: the subtitles and chapters as timed rows, to
  generate again or delete.
- **Sharing**: who can watch (signed or public), share links, and playback
  IDs. A public video shows its stream URL and a `<clipmux-player>`
  snippet.
- **Analytics**: views, watch time and other metrics for this video.

From the video's header you can rename it, re-encode it (with a choice of
resolutions) or delete it.

## Upload

Choose or drop several files at once. Each shows its progress, can be
paused or cancelled, and resumes after a reload. Before uploading you choose
who can watch, whether to generate subtitles and chapters, which
resolutions to encode, and, under advanced options, the app and a
reference of your own. Chapters need an LLM connected under
**Captions & AI**.

## Analytics

Views, watch time, unique viewers and bandwidth for the last 24 hours, 7,
30 or 90 days, compared with the period before, and the top videos. Filter
by app. See [Views, watch time and unique viewers](/docs/build/analytics)
for what counts.

## Jobs

Every job: encodes, re-encodes, imports, subtitles, chapters, moves and
deletes, filtered by status and kind. A row opens into its attempts: where
each ran, why it was routed there, which encoder each output used and any
that failed, and the error.

## Encoding

![The Encoding page: this server, Modal and RunPod as choices](/screenshots/encoding-light.webp)

Where new videos are encoded: **This server**, **Modal** or **RunPod**.
Connect a cloud GPU here, see its spend this month against its limit, and
choose whether this server takes over when the cloud GPU can't. See
[Encode on this server](/docs/self-host/encoding/this-server) and
[Routing, fallback and spend limits](/docs/self-host/encoding/routing-and-limits).

## Storage

Where uploaded originals are kept: this server's disk, R2 buckets and
S3-compatible stores, with how many videos each holds and which is the
default. Add a bucket, rotate an S3 store's keys, remove one no video uses,
and move existing uploads to the default. See
[Where videos are stored](/docs/self-host/storage).

## Delivery

How viewers get videos: from this server, or from Cloudflare's edge with
encoded files in R2. Connect or disconnect Cloudflare, move existing
encoded files to R2, and connect edge analytics. See
[Built-in delivery](/docs/self-host/delivery/built-in) and
[Cloudflare edge delivery](/docs/self-host/delivery/cloudflare).

## Captions & AI

![Captions and AI: subtitles on your machines or a speech API, and an LLM for chapters](/screenshots/captions-light.webp)

Where subtitles are made, on machines you run or by a speech API you
connect, each choice saying what leaves your servers; and the LLM that
titles chapters. See
[Transcribe with whisper.cpp](/docs/self-host/captions-and-ai/whisper),
[Use a speech API](/docs/self-host/captions-and-ai/speech-apis) and
[Choose an LLM for chapters](/docs/self-host/captions-and-ai/chapters-llm).

## Apps & API

![Apps and API: an app's API keys, signing keys and webhooks](/screenshots/apps-light.webp)

One app per site or service that uses ClipMux. **Create app** shows its
secrets once. Each app has tabs for **API keys**, **Signing keys**,
**Webhooks** (with each endpoint's health and deliveries, redelivery, and
secret rotation) and a **Danger zone** to disable it. See the
[Quickstart](/docs/build/quickstart).

## Settings

- **General**: the public and media URLs, and the setup test.
- **Account**: your password and two-factor sign-in.
- **Security**: rotating the content key, and a reminder about backups.
- **About**: the licences. Run `clipmux version` on the server for the
  release.
