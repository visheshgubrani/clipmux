title: Transcribe with whisper.cpp
description: Make subtitles and transcripts on the machines that encode your videos, with a pinned whisper.cpp model and no speech API.
---

By default, ClipMux makes subtitles where your videos are encoded: the
built-in worker on your server, a PC you own, or a Modal or RunPod machine.
The worker runs whisper.cpp on the video's audio, inside the same sandbox as
ffmpeg, and sends the transcript back to the server. No speech API is
involved, and the dashboard's **Captions & AI** page calls this option
**Where videos are encoded**.

This page covers how a worker decides whether, and on what, it transcribes,
the model it uses, and how fast it is.

## Before you start

- A worker that encodes your videos: the built-in worker, a
  [PC you own](/docs/self-host/encoding/your-pc), or
  [Modal](/docs/self-host/encoding/modal) or
  [RunPod](/docs/self-host/encoding/runpod).
- Uploads that ask for subtitles. See
  [Subtitles and transcripts](/docs/build/captions/subtitles).

<Note>
  On Modal or RunPod, transcription runs on the provider's machine, which
  already has the video for encoding. The audio doesn't go anywhere the
  video hasn't.
</Note>

## Choose how a worker transcribes

`CLIPMUX_SPEECH` (or `clipmux worker --speech`) sets it. serve passes its
value to the built-in worker.

| Mode | What the worker does |
| --- | --- |
| `auto` | The default. Tries CUDA on an NVIDIA card, then Vulkan on any GPU, then the CPU if it has at least 4 cores and 6 GiB of memory. |
| `cuda` | CUDA only. |
| `vulkan` | Vulkan only. |
| `cpu` | The CPU only, whatever its size. |
| `off` | Doesn't transcribe. |

At start, the worker proves each candidate with a real transcription under
the sandbox, and uses the first that passes. A GPU passes only if whisper.cpp
reports that it actually ran on it, so a GPU that silently falls back to the
CPU fails its probe and the next candidate is tried. The worker's log says
which backend it chose, or why it won't transcribe.

On the CPU, whisper gets half the logical CPUs, leaving the rest to the
encoder.

### Where CUDA is available

- **The amd64 image** carries whisper.cpp with CUDA, built for cards from the
  T4 through the A10G, RTX A4000 and A5000, L4 and L40S, with PTX for newer
  ones. This is the image the built-in worker, Modal and RunPod run.
- **arm64 images** have no CUDA.
- **The PC agent tarball** leaves CUDA out (about 1 GB), so a PC's NVIDIA card
  uses Vulkan, or the CPU if its driver offers no Vulkan.
- **Modal** offers no Vulkan device, so CUDA is the only GPU path there.

## The model

Every worker uses the same pinned model:

- `ggml-large-v3-turbo-q8_0`, whisper large-v3-turbo quantized to 8 bits;
- Silero VAD, which skips the silence between speech.

Together they are about 0.9 GB. The image has them baked in, at
`/usr/share/clipmux/models` (`CLIPMUX_BAKED_MODELS_DIR`), so a Modal or
RunPod machine downloads nothing. A worker without them, such as a PC agent,
downloads them once from Hugging Face, checks each against its pinned
SHA-256, and keeps them in `CLIPMUX_MODELS_DIR` across restarts.

whisper.cpp detects the language of each video on its own. A video gets one
subtitles track, in one language.

## How fast it is

Speeds are given as a real-time factor: the time to transcribe divided by
the length of the audio. Lower is faster; 0.25 means an hour of audio takes
15 minutes.

| Where | Backend | Measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A 6-core desktop CPU, 6 threads | CPU | 0.40 on 2 minutes of English speech |
| Modal, NVIDIA T4 | CUDA | 0.14 on a 20-minute English video |
| Modal, NVIDIA L4 | CUDA | 0.23 on a 54-minute Hindi lecture |
| Modal, NVIDIA T4 | CUDA | 0.30 on a 67-minute Hindi lecture |

On an AMD RX 6600 XT over Vulkan, under the sandbox, a short test clip took
2.7 seconds against 22.8 seconds on the CPU. Short clips pay a fixed cost:
whisper works in 30-second windows, so an 11-second clip takes about 22
seconds on the CPU.

## Check it works

On a PC agent, `clipmux worker doctor` runs the same speech probe the worker
does and prints the result:

- `speech` with a ✓: whisper ran the model on the named backend.
- A note that the model isn't downloaded yet: the worker downloads it at
  start.
- A note that there's no `whisper-cli`: this worker won't transcribe.
- `speech` with a ✗: every candidate failed its probe; the line names each
  failure.

For any worker, upload a short video that asks for subtitles, and open the
**Captions & chapters** tab on its page in the dashboard. Run
`clipmux admin setup-test --wait` on the server while a worker that can
transcribe is online, and it checks subtitles too.
